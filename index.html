PD Topic Priorities (Q9)
Q9: Which of the following leadership or professional learning topics would you find most valuable in a future AAESQ PD program? · Select all that apply — respondents could choose multiple topics
📋 What Members Are Telling Us About PD Needs

145 active members selected from a list of PD topics. Results reveal five clear priority areas, with wellbeing and human-centred leadership dominating — followed by a strong and consistent call for AI readiness.

Wellbeing & Mental Health — top priority cluster (195 total selections)
Staff wellbeing & burnout prevention leads all topics at 85 responses (59% of respondents). Combined with student mental health (58) and emotional intelligence (52), wellbeing-related topics account for the highest demand of any category. Members are signalling that the human cost of school leadership is the most urgent PD frontier.
Instructional & Staff Leadership — second cluster (144 total selections)
Coaching & supervising staff (54), instructional leadership (47) and staff recruitment & retention (43) cluster together as core leadership competencies. These reflect the operational realities of principals managing staff shortages, difficult performance conversations and the need to lead learning in their buildings.
Technology & AI Readiness — third cluster (95 total selections)
AI integration & policy (53) and AI literacy & ethical use (42) together reach 95 selections — with nearly half of all respondents selecting at least one. This is a rapidly emerging area where members feel underprepared and are looking to AAESQ to take a leadership role in equipping English-language administrators.
Equity & Inclusion / Data & Strategy — fourth & fifth clusters
Inclusive education & support needs (51) and equity, diversity & inclusion (41) reflect the growing complexity of student populations in English schools. Data-driven decision making (43) and change management (35) round out the picture, signalling demand for strategic leadership tools, not just classroom-level support.
What the research says: The most effective professional development for school administrators combines three elements: individualized coaching and mentoring, active participation in peer learning communities, and applied, problem-based learning grounded in real school contexts (Edutopia / Developing Expert Principals, 2025; Vanderbilt / Wallace Foundation, 2026). A 2024 RAND randomized controlled trial found that principals who received both structured PD and coaching showed stronger gains in instructional leadership and school climate than either intervention alone. Learning Forward research (April 2024) confirms that peer dialogue — characterized by trust, reciprocity and shared challenges — is among the most effective and valued forms of professional learning for principals, particularly experienced ones. TALIS research identifies five features of effective principal CPD: content-focused, active learning, school-embedded, peer cooperation, and sustained duration. The AAESQ survey data aligns strongly with these findings: members prioritize coaching, peer networks and practical strategies over theoretical or lecture-based formats — and 72% say they would commit to a sustained 6-day program if offered.
